# Weak Supervision for Multi-View Human Pose Estimation

## 1. Problem statement

Accurate 3D human pose estimation from calibrated multi-view video usually relies on expensive 3D ground-truth (GT), e.g., marker-based mocap or synchronized multi-view setups with manual annotation. For CVPR/ICRA 2027, a compelling research direction is to relax this requirement: **train a multi-view fusion or SMPL-fitting module using only weak, cheap, or partial supervision**, such as:

- 2D keypoints or bounding boxes (no 3D labels);
- geometric constraints (epipolar consistency, bone-length priors, temporal smoothness);
- pseudo-3D labels generated by triangulation or a strong single-view estimator;
- mixed supervision where only a small fraction of frames/views have 3D GT.

In the MotionFlow multi-view pipeline, the central task is to fuse per-view 2D keypoints and confidences into a metric world-coordinate `HumanMotionIR` (SMPL pose). The current bottleneck is that all learned fusion plugins are trained either on synthetic 3D sequences or on real data with **DLT-triangulated pseudo-GT** (`experiments/train_attention_fusion_shelf.py`). This report asks: *How can weak, multi-view, and geometry-derived supervision be exploited more systematically to close the gap between learned fusion and the strong DLT baseline?*

## 2. Key related work / methods

### 2.1 EpipolarPose — self-supervision from multi-view geometry
Kocabas et al. (*Self-Supervised Learning of 3D Human Pose using Multi-view Geometry*, CVPR 2019) train a 2D-to-3D lifting network using only multi-view 2D poses. They exploit **epipolar constraints** between views and enforce that lifted 3D points re-project consistently across views. No 3D GT is required. This is directly analogous to our reprojection-loss training, but their network learns from raw images rather than pre-extracted 2D keypoints.

### 2.2 TriPose — triangulation from video
Gholami et al. (*TriPose: A Weakly-Supervised 3D Human Pose Estimation via Triangulation from Video*, 2021) extend the idea to video. They estimate relative camera orientations, triangulate 3D poses from high-confidence 2D joints, and train a recurrent lifting network. A **multi-view reprojection loss** and cross-view consistency loss are the only supervisory signals. Their results suggest that temporal information and confidence filtering are crucial when 3D labels are absent.

### 2.3 Learnable triangulation with 3D GT
Iskakov et al. (*Learnable Triangulation of Human Pose*, ICCV 2019) show that with 3D GT, learned triangulation can outperform geometric baselines. Their method predicts per-view weights and performs differentiable algebraic triangulation. Our `RobustTriangulationModel` (`motionflow_mv/fusion/robust_triangulation.py`) follows the same principle, but current training lacks real 3D GT and therefore cannot demonstrate a clear gain over DLT.

### 2.4 Weakly-supervised cross-view graph networks
Hua et al. (*Weakly-supervised 3D Human Pose Estimation with Cross-view U-shaped Graph Convolutional Network*, IEEE TMM 2022) use **cross-view geometric consistency** and a graph structure to propagate 2D evidence. Their approach highlights the value of **skeleton topology** as a weak prior—something our current attention models ignore.

### 2.5 Geometric self-supervision
Chen et al. (*Unsupervised 3D Pose Estimation with Geometric Self-Supervision*, 2019) learn 3D pose from unlabeled multi-view images by enforcing that predictions satisfy **bone-length constancy** and **viewpoint consistency**. These constraints are free and can be added to any reprojection-based loss.

## 3. Relation to the current MotionFlow multi-view codebase

The repository already implements many building blocks of a weakly-supervised multi-view system:

| Component | File | Current role | Weak-supervision opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| DLT triangulation | `motionflow_mv/fusion/triangulation.py` | Baseline and pseudo-GT generator | Already the “teacher” for pseudo-labels; can be upgraded with confidence filtering and epipolar validation |
| Attention fusion | `motionflow_mv/fusion/attention_model.py`, `attention_model_v2.py` | Learned 2D → 3D fusion | Trained on synthetic 3D or DLT pseudo-GT; can add reprojection + bone-length + symmetry losses |
| Robust triangulation | `motionflow_mv/fusion/robust_triangulation.py` | Differentiable DLT with learned weights | Currently trained with reprojection loss only; could add geometric self-supervision |
| Residual refiner | `motionflow_mv/fusion/residual_refiner.py` | Post-DLT residual correction | Same as above; temporal and bone-length priors could regularize |
| Synthetic dataset | `motionflow_mv/data/synthetic_3d_dataset.py` | 3D-supervised pre-training | Domain gap to real data; needs noise/occlusion/camera distribution matching |
| Shelf training | `experiments/train_attention_fusion_shelf.py` | DLT pseudo-GT fine-tuning | Pseudo-label quality limits ceiling; needs uncertainty-weighted loss and label-cleaning |

Key observations from `docs/design_v3.md` and `README.md`:

1. **DLT is the dominant baseline.** All learned variants trained only with reprojection/pseudo-GT fail to beat DLT on Shelf (mean ~9.9 px vs. attention ~80 px).
2. **Cross-dataset generalization is poor.** Shelf-trained `attention` does not transfer to Campus without fine-tuning.
3. **3D GT is the missing ingredient.** `design_v2.md` already identifies Human3.6M, CMU Panoptic, and 3DPW as targets, but none are currently integrated.

Weak supervision is therefore not a luxury but a necessity: the project needs to train stronger fusion models **before** or **while** acquiring full 3D GT.

## 4. Concrete recommendations

### 4.1 Immediate: upgrade the reprojection-only loss to a weak-supervised geometry loss

Replace the simple MSE/reprojection loss in `train_attention_fusion_shelf.py` with:

\[
\mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\text{rep}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{reproj}} + \lambda_{\text{bone}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{bone}} + \lambda_{\text{sym}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{symmetry}} + \lambda_{\text{temp}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{temporal}} + \lambda_{\text{conf}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{confidence}}
\]

- **Reprojection loss** `L_reproj`: project fused 3D joints back to each view and compare with 2D detections.
- **Bone-length consistency** `L_bone`: penalize deviation from a learned or SMPL skeleton prior.
- **Symmetry loss** `L_symmetry`: left/right limb lengths should match.
- **Temporal smoothness** `L_temporal`: second-order finite difference of 3D joints.
- **Confidence loss** `L_confidence`: encourage the network’s predicted view weights to correlate with reprojection residual.

This requires no new data and directly addresses the “no 3D GT” constraint.

### 4.2 Short-term: implement epipolar consistency for pseudo-label filtering

Before training, filter the DLT pseudo-GT:

1. For each joint, compute the **epipolar residual** across view pairs.
2. Reject frames/joints where epipolar consistency is poor.
3. Use the filtered subset for fine-tuning `AttentionFusionModelV2`.

This is a direct application of EpipolarPose/TriPose ideas to our 2D-keypoint fusion setting.

### 4.3 Short-term: use ScoreHMR as a pseudo-3D teacher

`docs/design_v2.md` already proposes ScoreHMR as a per-view plugin. Concretely:

1. Run ScoreHMR on each view to get per-view SMPL parameters.
2. Project ScoreHMR joints to 2D and use them as higher-quality pseudo-labels for training the fusion head.
3. Alternatively, lift ScoreHMR’s per-view 3D estimates to world coordinates and use them as soft 3D targets.

This provides pseudo-3D supervision without manual 3D annotation.

### 4.4 Medium-term: synthetic-to-real domain adaptation

`motionflow_mv/data/synthetic_3d_dataset.py` currently generates random skeletons. Upgrade it to:

- Sample poses from **AMASS** or at least enforce plausible kinematic limits.
- Match real camera distributions (focal length, baseline, height).
- Add realistic 2D detector noise and **occlusion masks**.
- Use domain-adversarial or feature-alignment loss when fine-tuning on real Shelf/Campus.

### 4.5 Medium-term: semi-supervised mixing of 3D GT and weak labels

Once Human3.6M or Panoptic 3D GT is available, train with a small amount of real 3D GT and a large amount of weakly-labeled in-the-wild video. The weak labels can come from 2D detectors plus the geometry losses above. This is the realistic path to a CVPR/ICRA-scale dataset.

### 4.6 Evaluation protocol

Add weak-supervision-specific diagnostics:

- **Pseudo-label accuracy**: MPJPE of DLT/ScoreHMR pseudo-labels vs. any available 3D GT.
- **Reprojection error** (px): retained for sanity.
- **MPJPE / PA-MPJPE** (mm) on Human3.6M S9/S11 once integrated.
- **Bone-length consistency** and **temporal jitter** as weak-supervision quality indicators.
- **Ablation**: reprojection-only vs. reprojection + bone + symmetry + temporal.

## 5. Open questions / risks

| Risk / question | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Pseudo-label bias** | DLT pseudo-GT inherits 2D detector errors and camera noise; training on it may amplify bias | Filter by epipolar residual; use ScoreHMR pseudo-3D; mix with real 3D GT when available |
| **Over-smoothing from bone/symmetry losses** | Strong geometric priors can collapse pose estimates to an average skeleton | Weight losses carefully; validate on 3D GT; use pose-dependent bone-length priors |
| **Cross-dataset generalization** | Current Shelf-trained models fail on Campus | Scale/camera-invariant architecture; domain adaptation; train on diverse synthetic rigs |
| **Lack of 3D GT validation** | Weak-supervised improvements are hard to measure without real 3D labels | Prioritize Human3.6M registration and loader integration |
| **Computational cost of ScoreHMR** | Running diffusion-based single-view inference per view is slow | Batch on A800-D; use only for offline pseudo-labeling, not online inference |
| **SMPL fitting from weak 3D** | The pipeline ultimately needs SMPL parameters, not just joints | Add a weakly-supervised SMPL fitting step on top of fused joints (e.g., via SMPLify with 2D reprojection loss) |

## 6. Conclusion

Weak supervision is the natural next step for the MotionFlow multi-view extension because the project currently has strong geometric baselines and a modular plugin architecture but lacks abundant 3D GT. By combining **reprojection loss**, **epipolar consistency**, **bone-length/symmetry priors**, and **temporal smoothness**, the learned fusion plugins can be trained without expensive 3D labels. ScoreHMR provides a ready source of pseudo-3D supervision, while synthetic data and domain adaptation can bridge the gap to real datasets. The most actionable next step is to implement the combined weak-supervised loss and epipolar pseudo-label filtering for `AttentionFusionModelV2`, then validate on the first available real 3D-GT benchmark (Human3.6M).
